Becoming Self-Employed in Germany

Many people from Ukraine ran their own business before the war: a beauty salon, an IT company, a translation service, a small shop. In Germany you can work for yourself too. This guide explains the formal steps and the traps to avoid.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

Are you allowed to be self-employed?

Yes. A residence permit for temporary protection under §24 allows employment and self-employment from the first day. The permit states that gainful employment is allowed (Erwerbstätigkeit erlaubt). Other residence permits have their own rules, so check yours if you change permit later.

Trade or freelance profession?

German law distinguishes two forms of self-employment:

  • Trade (Gewerbe): most businesses, such as shops, cleaning services, cosmetics, catering, transport or online trade. You must register the trade at the town hall.
  • Freelance profession (freier Beruf): for example translators, interpreters, IT consultants, teachers, artists, journalists, doctors or architects. You do not register a trade, only inform the tax office.

If you are unsure which applies, the tax office or the chamber of commerce can tell you.

Crafts that need a master's qualification

Some crafts may only be run independently by people with a master craftsman qualification (Meister) or an equivalent recognised qualification, for example electricians, plumbers, hairdressers or car mechanics. Other crafts are free to start. Before registering, ask the chamber of crafts for Upper Franconia whether your craft requires a master title and whether your Ukrainian qualification can be recognised for it.

Registering a trade (Gewerbeanmeldung)

You register your trade at the trade office (Gewerbeamt) of your town, usually in person or online, for a small fee. You need your passport, residence permit and registration certificate. The trade office automatically informs the tax office, the chamber and other authorities. You then receive a questionnaire from the tax office, or you fill it in yourself online.

The tax office questionnaire

Every self-employed person submits the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through the ELSTER portal. You estimate your expected turnover and profit and choose how VAT will be handled. The tax office then assigns you a tax number (Steuernummer), which you must put on every invoice. Take this form seriously; wrong estimates lead to high advance tax payments.

VAT and the small business rule

Normally, businesses add VAT (Umsatzsteuer) to their invoices and pass it on to the tax office. If your turnover stays below a legal limit, you can use the small business rule (Kleinunternehmerregelung) and invoice without VAT. This simplifies bookkeeping, but you cannot deduct VAT on purchases. A tax adviser or the chamber can help you decide.

Health insurance and pension

Self-employed people must take care of their own health insurance. If you receive Jobcenter benefits while starting, the Jobcenter usually continues to insure you. Without benefits, you insure yourself, typically as a voluntary member of a statutory fund, with contributions based on income. Pension insurance is voluntary for most self-employed people, but compulsory for some professions such as certain craftspeople and teachers. Plan these costs from the start.

Important: health insurance contributions for self-employed people can be a large monthly cost. Calculate them before you give up other income.

Starting from Jobcenter benefits

If you receive Jobcenter benefits, tell your caseworker about your plan before you start. The Jobcenter may support a promising business with a start-up allowance (Einstiegsgeld) or a grant for equipment, usually after checking a business plan. Your earnings are reported monthly and partly counted against your benefits. Starting without informing the Jobcenter leads to repayment demands.

Your business plan

A business plan describes what you offer, who your customers are, what it costs and how much you expect to earn. Even if nobody asks for it, writing one helps you see whether the idea works in Upper Franconia. Include costs such as rent, insurance, health insurance, materials, phone and travel. The chambers and start-up advisory services check business plans free of charge.

Bookkeeping and invoices

Keep all receipts and write proper invoices with your name, address, tax number, invoice number, date, description of the service and amount. Small businesses usually use a simple income-surplus calculation (Einnahmen-Überschuss-Rechnung) for the annual tax return. Simple bookkeeping apps help; for more complex cases, a tax adviser (Steuerberater) is worth the cost.

Rules for typical businesses

  • Food and catering: hygiene training and food safety rules apply; ask the food inspection authority of the district.
  • Beauty and cosmetics: some services count as regulated crafts; check with the chamber of crafts.
  • Transport and delivery: commercial passenger or goods transport needs special licences.
  • Online work for Ukrainian clients: you are taxed in Germany if you live here; keep invoices and payment records.

Insurance and contracts

Business liability insurance protects you if you damage a customer's property or cause injury. Use written contracts or order confirmations, especially for larger jobs. Never sign leases for business premises before you have checked the costs with an adviser.

Finding customers

In small towns, word of mouth matters most. Tell neighbours, the school community and local groups about your service. A Google Business profile with opening hours and photos, a simple website and a presence in local Facebook groups help people find you. Print a few flyers for notice boards in supermarkets. Many Ukrainian entrepreneurs start with clients from the Ukrainian community and then expand to German customers once their German improves.

A separate business account

Keep business and private money apart. A separate account, even a simple second current account, makes bookkeeping and the tax return much easier, and shows the Jobcenter clearly what you earn. Card terminals and online payment services charge fees; compare them before you sign up.

Common mistakes

  • Starting before registering the trade or informing the tax office and Jobcenter.
  • Forgetting health insurance contributions and income tax when calculating prices.
  • Not keeping receipts, which makes the tax return expensive later.
  • Opening a regulated craft without checking the master requirement.
  • Signing long contracts for premises, leasing or software too early.

Free advice in Upper Franconia

The chamber of industry and commerce (IHK) and the chamber of crafts (HWK) for Upper Franconia offer free start-up counselling, seminars and information in several languages. The federal start-up portal explains every step online. Migration counselling services can connect you with mentors who have started businesses themselves.

Frequently asked questions

Can I be self-employed with a §24 permit?

Yes. Self-employment is allowed from the first day.

Do I need to register a trade?

For most businesses yes, at the trade office of your town. Freelance professions only inform the tax office.

Do I need health insurance as a self-employed person?

Yes. Health insurance is compulsory; without Jobcenter benefits you insure yourself.

Can the Jobcenter support my start-up?

It can, for example with a start-up allowance, after checking your plan. Tell your caseworker before you start.

Which crafts need a master title?

Some crafts, for example electricians, plumbers or hairdressers. Ask the chamber of crafts.
