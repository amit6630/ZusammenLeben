Orientation Guide for Families from Ukraine

Clear, straightforward explanations of essential German terms, procedures, and official requirements you will encounter during your first year.

General Information Notice: This guide is designed to help you understand the overall landscape before starting procedures. Rules and regulations vary across cities, local authorities, and individual residency statuses, and are updated periodically. This content does not constitute legal advice or a substitute for direct verification with competent official authorities.

01 Registration and Public Authorities

Anmeldung — Address Registration

Mandatory First Step

What it is: The official declaration of your residential address to the local municipality (Bürgeramt / Einwohnermeldeamt).

Why it matters: Without address registration, virtually no other administrative step can proceed — including health insurance, opening a bank account, social benefits, or school enrollment.

Basic Steps

Book an appointment at the local municipal office, attend with required documents, and receive your official registration certificate (Meldebestätigung).

Common Documents

Valid passport/ID card, landlord confirmation of move-in (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung), and completed registration form.

Common Mistakes: Delaying registration beyond required deadlines, attending without the landlord's signed confirmation, or failing to update your registration when moving apartments.
When to contact us: When requirements are unclear, documents are missing, or available appointment dates are too far in the future.

Jobcenter

Living Expenses and Housing

What it is: The government authority responsible for managing living allowance assistance, housing cost coverage, and employment integration support for eligible individuals.

Why it matters: In many cases, the Jobcenter must pre-approve housing expenses before a rental agreement is finalized, making them a key stakeholder in your relocation process.

Basic Steps

Initial registration/application submission, submitting verified supporting documentation, attending scheduled appointments, and tracking official written communications.

Common Mistakes

Failing to reply to official letters within stated deadlines, not reporting changes in personal circumstances, or assuming that receiving no response means everything is fine.

Important to understand: All final decisions rest exclusively with the Jobcenter. Our role is to explain requirements and accompany you through the process, not to make administrative determinations.
When to contact us: When you receive official letters you cannot understand, before critical appointments, or during the apartment approval phase.

Apartment Approval (Wohnungsbeschaffung)

Crucial Step

What it is: Official pre-approval confirming that a prospective rental apartment meets designated standards regarding total size and rental price limits.

Why it matters: Signing a lease contract before receiving formal approval may leave you personally liable for rental commitments that are not covered by social allowances.

Golden Rule: Never sign a lease agreement before your apartment approval status is fully confirmed.
When to contact us: Always contact us before signing the contract. Reversing an executed lease is extremely difficult.

02 Education and Childcare

School Enrollment (Schule)

Basic Steps: Apply through the local education authority or directly at the designated school, present required documents, attend an assessment interview, and evaluate language placement needs.

Common Documents: Address registration certificate (Meldebestätigung), parent and child identity documents, official vaccination records (measles immunization), and previous school records if available.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the formal start of the school year instead of contacting educational authorities early to explore language support classes (Willkommensklassen).

Kindergarten (Kita)

What to know: Childcare places are strictly limited, and long waiting lists exist in many municipalities. It is strongly advised to apply as early as possible and register across multiple institutions simultaneously whenever permitted.

When to contact us: At the very start of your search, before encountering multiple rejections.

03 Financial, Medical and Daily Services

Health Insurance

Health insurance is mandatory in Germany. Without it, access to medical services is severely restricted. Keep your insurance card safe and inform your insurer immediately of any address or family status changes.

Bank Account

Necessary for virtually all financial transactions — receiving support/salary, paying rent, and utility bills. Requires a valid identity document and your address registration certificate (Meldebestätigung).

Public Transit

Upper Franconia features a regional bus and train network. In smaller towns, schedules run less frequently than in big cities — advance planning is crucial for official appointments.

Emergency Numbers in Germany

Dial 112 for Medical Emergencies and Fire Department.
Dial 110 for Police assistance.

04 Housing Contracts and Utilities

Lease Agreements and Security Deposits

Key factors before signing: Understand the exact difference between basic rent (Kaltmiete) and total rent including heating/utilities (Warmmiete). Verify what is included, the security deposit amount (Kaution), repayment conditions, and mandatory lease cancellation notice periods.

Common Mistakes: Signing a binding lease without full comprehension, failing to document apartment handover condition (Übergabeprotokoll) upon move-in, or transferring cash deposits without an official written receipt.
When to contact us: Always before signing. Once signed, lease terms are legally binding and cannot be easily modified.

Electricity, Gas and Heating

In most cases, residents sign separate individual contracts with utility providers. Monthly payments are estimated advance instalments; at year-end, actual consumption is calculated, resulting in either a refund or an additional payment balance.

Tip: Always record meter readings on move-in day and update your address with utility suppliers.

Internet and Telecom

Many German telecom contracts carry fixed minimum terms (often up to 24 months) and renew automatically. Pay close attention to contract cancellation deadlines and notice periods prior to signing.

05 Mail, Integration and Rights

Golden Rule for Official Letters in Germany

In Germany, an official letter is never a suggestion. Letters almost always contain strict response deadlines, and non-response is legally interpreted as tacit agreement or refusal.

  • Open all mail immediately upon receipt.
  • Check for explicit response deadlines or appointment dates.
  • Keep a copy of every document sent or received.
  • If you do not understand a letter: Take a clear photo and send it to our team immediately.

German Language Courses

Language is the ultimate key to independence. Integration courses (Integrationskurs) and language classes are available, with eligibility depending on your legal status and local municipality.

Employment & Recognition

Job market integration depends heavily on language proficiency and formal recognition of Ukrainian qualifications/diplomas. Start qualification recognition procedures early.

Doctors and Pharmacies

Start by seeing a general practitioner (Hausarzt), who issues referrals to specialists. Pharmacies (Apotheken) operate regular hours, with scheduled emergency services (Notdienst) at night and weekends.

06 Rights, Obligations and Record Keeping

Tenant Rights and Obligations

Tenants in Germany enjoy strong legal protections, but also have strict responsibilities: punctual rent payment, property care, adhering to quiet hours (Ruhezeiten), and proper waste separation. Understanding these rules prevents most disputes.

Address Change & Archiving

Whenever you move, you must notify the registration office, Jobcenter, health insurer, bank, and service providers. Maintain a physical folder with all original documents and keep digital photo copies on your phone.
