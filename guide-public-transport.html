Getting Around: Public Transport in Upper Franconia

In rural Northern Bavaria, getting from A to B takes some planning. Buses and regional trains connect most towns, and with the right ticket travel is affordable. This guide explains tickets, apps and the rules that newcomers often do not know.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

How transport works in our region

Upper Franconia is largely rural. Regional trains connect the bigger towns such as Hof, Münchberg, Marktredwitz, Selb, Kulmbach and Bayreuth. Buses connect villages and smaller towns, often run by the districts or private bus companies on their behalf. Bus timetables are aligned with school times: on school days there are more buses, during holidays and at weekends there are fewer, and in the evenings some lines stop running. Always check the timetable for the exact day.

Planning your journey

The DB Navigator app of Deutsche Bahn shows almost all trains and many buses in Germany, with live delays. Regional transport associations and bus companies also have their own apps and printed timetables. Enter your start and destination, the date and time, and the app shows connections, transfers and ticket options. Save important connections, such as the way to work or the course, as favourites.

The Deutschland-Ticket

The Deutschland-Ticket is a monthly subscription for all local and regional public transport in Germany: buses, trams, underground and regional trains (RE, RB, S-Bahn). It does not cover long-distance trains (ICE, IC, EC) or long-distance buses. The price is set nationally and adjusted from time to time. It is a digital ticket on your phone or a chip card, personal and not transferable, and can be cancelled monthly.

For anyone who commutes regularly or travels several times a month, it is usually far cheaper than single tickets. Many employers subsidise it as a job ticket, and discounted versions exist for some groups, such as students, trainees or volunteers in some regions. Ask your employer, school or the Jobcenter.

Tip: the Deutschland-Ticket is a subscription. If you no longer need it, cancel in time before the end of the month, otherwise it renews automatically.

Single and day tickets

If you travel only occasionally, buy single or day tickets. Train tickets for regional trains are sold in the app, online or at ticket machines at the station. On many trains in Bavaria there is no ticket sale on board, so you must buy the ticket before boarding. On buses you can often buy tickets from the driver, sometimes only by card or app. Group and day tickets can be good value for families.

Travelling without a valid ticket

Ticket inspectors check regularly. Travelling without a valid ticket costs an increased fare (erhöhtes Beförderungsentgelt), currently a fixed amount of several dozen euros, plus possible criminal proceedings for repeated fare dodging. If your digital ticket does not load, show a screenshot and your ID; keep your phone charged.

Children and young people

Children under six usually travel free when accompanied. Older children need their own ticket or a reduced fare; family day tickets help. Pupils in Bavaria often have free transport to school if the distance is above a certain limit; the school or the district arranges this. Ask at the school office.

On-demand buses and call taxis

In rural areas, some lines run only on request: a call bus (Rufbus) or shared call taxi (Anruf-Sammel-Taxi) operates on a timetable but only if you book by phone or app, often at least an hour in advance. These services are part of public transport and accept normal tickets or charge a small extra. The timetable marks such trips with a telephone symbol.

Bicycles

A bicycle is a good option for short distances, and many towns have improved cycle paths. Taking a bike on regional trains usually requires a separate bike ticket, and space is limited at peak times. Lock your bicycle well; bike theft happens in stations. Lights and brakes must work, as the police check this.

Long-distance travel

For trips to big cities, long-distance trains (ICE/IC) are fast but expensive at the last minute. Saver fares (Sparpreis) bought early are much cheaper, and a BahnCard gives discounts for frequent travellers. Long-distance buses are often the cheapest option, for example to Munich, Berlin or towards the Polish and Czech borders. Buses and trains to Ukraine run from several German cities; book early, especially before holidays.

Taxis and car-sharing

Taxis are available in the larger towns, but expensive for long distances; ask the price before the trip. For hospital visits and medical treatment, health insurance can in some cases pay for transport if a doctor prescribes it in advance. Private car-sharing through neighbours, colleagues or the Ukrainian community is common in rural areas; agree on sharing petrol costs. Some towns also have volunteer driving services for older people.

A typical example: from a village to Hof

Imagine you live in a village near Helmbrechts and start work in Hof at 7 am. Check the evening before: is there an early bus to Helmbrechts or Münchberg, and a train connection from there? If the first bus is too late, ask about a call bus, a colleague who drives the same way, or a bicycle to the next station. Test the route once before the first working day. Many employers adjust shift times when you explain the transport situation.

Accessibility and help

If you travel with a wheelchair, pram or heavy luggage, many stations have lifts and staff can help if you book assistance in advance through the railway's mobility service. Low-floor buses can lower their entrance. People with a severe disability pass and a free-travel stamp can use local transport free of charge.

Costs and the Jobcenter

Transport costs are part of the standard benefit amount, but in some situations the Jobcenter can pay additional travel costs, for example for journeys to job interviews, to a course it has assigned, or at the start of a new job. Ask in advance and keep tickets as proof.

Strikes, delays and disruptions

Strikes and construction work occasionally disrupt train services. Apps show replacement buses (Schienenersatzverkehr) and delays. If a train is significantly late, you may be entitled to compensation for longer journeys; for the Deutschland-Ticket the rules are limited. Plan a buffer for important appointments, such as job interviews or exams.

How we help

We help you plan routes to work, school and appointments, explain ticket options, and connect people for car-sharing in villages with few buses. Ask for help.

Frequently asked questions

What does the Deutschland-Ticket cover?

All local and regional public transport in Germany, but not long-distance trains like ICE or IC.

Can I buy a ticket on the train?

On many regional trains in Bavaria no. Buy it before boarding, in the app or at the machine.

What if there is no bus in the evening?

Check for call buses or shared call taxis, which run on request if you book in advance.

Is school transport free?

In Bavaria often yes, if the distance exceeds a limit. Ask the school office.

What happens without a valid ticket?

You pay an increased fare of several dozen euros, and repeated fare dodging can be prosecuted.

Our volunteers answer in Ukrainian, Russian, German and English. Free of charge.
