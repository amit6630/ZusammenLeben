Q&A — Guide for Newcomers

Everything we were asked, organized by topic — with references to official authorities

Important Clarification: The answers provided are based on practical experience in the region. They do not constitute legal advice. Procedures, financial amounts, and eligibilities vary between cities and authorities, and are updated periodically. Always verify details with the competent official authority.

Part F — Status, Authorities & Documents

Because almost everything depends on it: health insurance, bank accounts, Jobcenter registration, enrolling children in school, and sometimes even securing a lease. Delaying your address registration delays everything else.

Contact the Foreigners Authority (Ausländerbehörde) at least eight weeks in advance. An expired permit creates a chain reaction of problems: stopped payments, health insurance issues, and employment blocks. Do not wait until the last minute.

It is a temporary certificate confirming that your application for a residence permit or extension is legally under review. It serves as a valid official document for most purposes, but be sure to monitor its expiration date as well.

A residence permit under § 24 AufenthG grants full access to the labor market — without needing separate approval and without restrictions on the type of work. This is a significant advantage compared to standard asylum procedures.

In Germany, you have a legal right to a basic bank account (Basiskonto) — every retail bank is required by law to offer one. If a bank refuses, demand a written rejection notice and file a complaint with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Part G — Children & Education

We assist in locating suitable educational settings, understanding documents, translation, and coordination. The actual school placement itself is determined by local authorities and the schools.

Yes. In Germany, schooling is mandatory (Schulpflicht) and authorities enforce it strictly. An unregistered child creates legal complications with authorities. It is not just a right — it is an obligation.

Most schools have integration classes for students who do not speak German. In Bavaria, they are usually called Deutschklasse or Übergangsklasse. Ask about this explicitly during registration.

It depends on the city, and wait times can often take several months. While children have a legal right to a spot starting from age one, availability is limited in practice. Register early and apply to several facilities simultaneously.

Yes — the Education and Participation package (Bildung und Teilhabe / BuT): covers school supplies, school lunches, transport, tutoring, and a allowance for sports or cultural clubs. It requires a separate application via the Jobcenter or local authority, and many families miss out on it.

Kindergeld is a child benefit paid monthly for each child. Applications are submitted to the Familienkasse of the Federal Employment Agency.

• Official portal: www.arbeitsagentur.de/familie-und-kinder
• Hotline: 0800 4 5555 30

Contact us — often a translated conversation with the school staff is enough to resolve the issue. Additionally, every school has an advisory teacher (Beratungslehrer), and every district offers an educational psychological support service (Schulpsychologischer Dienst).

Part H — Health & Medical

In an emergency, you will receive medical treatment regardless. Do not avoid going to the emergency room because of paperwork. However, you must arrange your health insurance coverage as soon as possible afterwards.

Statutory health insurance covers family doctors (GPs), specialists (often with a referral), hospital stays, prescription medications, emergency treatment, pregnancy and childbirth care, vaccinations, and routine health check-ups.

Yes. Bürgergeld recipients are entitled to an exemption from co-payments (Zuzahlungsbefreiung). You must submit an application for this directly to your health insurance fund (Krankenkasse).

In complex medical situations, funding for an interpreter can sometimes be approved. Eligibility varies by case and health insurance fund, so it is best to check with your Krankenkasse in advance. We can also assist in connecting you with a volunteer interpreter.

Call the national on-call medical service: 116 117 — for non-life-threatening medical care outside regular clinic hours. For life-threatening emergencies, dial 112 immediately.

Pharmacies operate on a regional emergency duty rotation system (Apotheken-Notdienst). The current schedule is posted on the front door of every pharmacy and is also available on official local pharmacy websites.

Seeking support is completely normal and valid, and many people in your situation need help. Your first point of contact is your family doctor (GP), who can provide a referral for therapy or mental health care. In cases of acute mental health distress or crisis, contact emergency services directly.
