Living with a Disability in Germany

People with disabilities have strong rights in Germany, and many forms of support exist, from tax relief and free transport to help at school and work. A Ukrainian disability group is not transferred automatically, so knowing the German procedure is important.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

Ukrainian disability status in Germany

A disability group certified in Ukraine is not automatically recognised in Germany. To receive German disability benefits, a new assessment is needed. Your Ukrainian medical documents are very helpful for this: bring every diagnosis, hospital report and certificate you have, ideally with translations of the key parts. Your family doctor and specialists in Germany will also issue new reports.

The degree of disability (GdB)

In Germany, the extent of a disability is measured as the degree of disability (Grad der Behinderung, GdB), from 20 to 100 in steps of ten. From a GdB of 50, a person counts as severely disabled (schwerbehindert) and receives the severe disability pass (Schwerbehindertenausweis). People with a GdB of 30 or 40 can, under certain conditions, be treated as equal to severely disabled people at work.

Where to apply in Bavaria

In Bavaria, the Bavarian Centre for Family and Social Affairs (Zentrum Bayern Familie und Soziales, ZBFS) decides on disability status; for Upper Franconia its regional office is responsible. You apply with a form, online or on paper, list your health problems and the doctors treating you, and allow the office to request medical reports. The decision usually takes several months.

Tip: describe all health problems, not only the main one. Several smaller impairments together can lead to a higher GdB. Ask your doctors to describe concrete limitations in daily life, not only diagnoses.

Marks on the pass (Merkzeichen)

Additional marks on the pass show specific needs and give access to further support:

  • G: significantly impaired mobility in road traffic; can give free local transport.
  • aG: extraordinary walking disability; allows disabled parking spaces.
  • B: a companion travels free on public transport.
  • H: helplessness; important for children and tax relief.
  • Bl, Gl, TBl: blindness, deafness, deafblindness.

Benefits of the severe disability pass

  • Tax allowances depending on the GdB, also for parents of children with disabilities.
  • Free or cheaper local public transport with a token (Wertmarke), depending on the marks.
  • At work: five extra days of holiday, special protection against dismissal, and support to adapt the workplace.
  • Discounts for museums, swimming pools, events and some memberships.
  • Earlier retirement under certain conditions.

Children with disabilities

For young children, early intervention (Frühförderung) offers therapy and support, such as physiotherapy, speech therapy and educational support, often at home or in special centres, free for the family. Kitas can provide integration places with extra staff. At school, parents can choose between inclusion in a regular school, sometimes with a school companion (Schulbegleitung), and special schools (Förderschulen) with smaller classes. The school office and the youth welfare office or district social services advise.

Integration assistance (Eingliederungshilfe)

People with significant disabilities can receive integration assistance (Eingliederungshilfe), which covers support for participation in everyday life, education and work, for example assistance at school, assisted living or support at work. In Bavaria, the district authorities (Bezirk Oberfranken) are responsible for many of these services. The procedure is detailed; counselling helps.

Work and disability

The integration specialist service (Integrationsfachdienst) supports people with disabilities in finding and keeping jobs and advises employers. The employment agency has rehabilitation teams for vocational training and retraining. Employers receive subsidies for hiring severely disabled people and adapting workplaces. Sheltered workshops (Werkstätten für behinderte Menschen) offer work for people who cannot work on the general labour market.

Benefits and extra needs

People with disabilities who receive Jobcenter or social welfare benefits may be entitled to additional amounts (Mehrbedarf), for example with the mark G or during vocational rehabilitation. Medical aids such as wheelchairs, hearing aids or orthopaedic devices are prescribed by doctors and paid by health insurance. Care needs are covered by care insurance; see our guide to care.

Free independent counselling (EUTB)

Supplementary independent participation counselling (EUTB) is free and advises people with disabilities and their families on all questions, independent of authorities. Many counsellors have disabilities themselves. EUTB centres exist in every region; some offer interpreters.

Accessibility in everyday life

Many public buildings, stations and buses are accessible. For train journeys, the railway's mobility service helps with boarding if booked in advance. Disabled parking spaces require a special permit linked to the mark aG. If you face barriers or discrimination, counselling services and disability associations can support you.

If the decision is wrong

If the GdB seems too low or a mark is missing, you can object within one month. Ask for the medical reports used in the decision and add missing documents. Social associations such as VdK or SoVD help their members with objections and court cases, which are free for insured people at the social court.

Arriving with a disability: the first steps

After arrival, register your address, get health insurance and see a family doctor as soon as possible. The doctor can refer you to specialists and write the reports the ZBFS needs. If you depend on medicines or aids, bring a list of them with active ingredients, because brand names differ between countries. Tell the immigration office and the Jobcenter about the disability early: it can matter for housing, for the type of job offered and for extra needs.

Accessible housing

Accessible flats are rare and in high demand. When you search, mention the disability and the pass, because some municipal and cooperative flats are reserved for people with special needs. Care insurance can pay up to several thousand euros for adapting a flat, for example a walk-in shower or a ramp. Social services and EUTB centres help with applications.

Parents and carers

Parents of children with disabilities and family carers carry a heavy load. Self-help groups for parents exist for many diagnoses, sometimes with Ukrainian- or Russian-speaking members. Relief services (Familienentlastende Dienste) look after the child for some hours so parents can rest. Ask about them at the Lebenshilfe or Caritas in your district.

How we help

We help families collect and translate medical documents, fill in applications to the ZBFS, find EUTB counselling and early intervention, and prepare for school and Kita conversations. Ask for help.

Frequently asked questions

Is my Ukrainian disability group valid in Germany?

Not automatically. A new German assessment is needed; your Ukrainian medical documents help.

Where do I apply in Bavaria?

At the Bavarian Centre for Family and Social Affairs (ZBFS), regional office for Upper Franconia.

From which GdB do I get the pass?

From a degree of disability of 50 you count as severely disabled and receive the pass.

Can I travel free on buses?

With certain marks such as G and a token (Wertmarke), local transport is free or cheaper.

Where can I get free advice?

At independent participation counselling (EUTB) centres, free and independent of authorities.

Need personal help?

Our volunteers answer in Ukrainian, Russian, German and English. Free of charge.
