Child Benefit and Family Payments in Germany

Germany supports families with several payments. Many Ukrainian parents receive less than they could, simply because nobody explained which office is responsible for what. This guide gives you the overview.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

An overview of family payments

Family support in Germany comes from different offices, and each has its own form. The most important are child benefit (Kindergeld) from the family benefits office, the child supplement (Kinderzuschlag) for working parents with low income, parental allowance (Elterngeld) after a birth, advance maintenance payments (Unterhaltsvorschuss) for single parents, and the education and participation package (Bildung und Teilhabe) for school costs. Not every family can receive all of them, and some count against others. The sections below explain each one.

Child benefit (Kindergeld)

Child benefit is a fixed monthly amount per child, paid regardless of income. Parents with a residence permit for temporary protection under §24 can receive it. It is usually paid until the child turns 18, and longer while the child is in school, vocational training or studies, in most cases up to 25. The amount is set by law and adjusted from time to time; the family benefits office publishes the current figure.

Who applies and where

Child benefit is paid to one parent only, usually the one the child lives with. You apply to the family benefits office (Familienkasse) of the Federal Employment Agency. The application can be submitted online or on paper. For the region of Upper Franconia, the responsible Familienkasse is shown on the application portal after you enter your address.

Documents for the child benefit application

  • Tax IDs (Steuer-ID) of the applying parent and of each child; they arrive by post after registration
  • Passports or birth certificates of the children
  • Your residence permit or provisional certificate
  • Registration certificate of the household
  • Bank details (IBAN) of an account in your name
  • For children over 18: proof of school, training or studies

How long it takes

Processing can take several weeks. Child benefit can be paid retroactively for a limited period, usually six months before the month of application. So apply as soon as you have the tax IDs, even if other documents are still missing; you can send them later. Keep the reference number (Kindergeldnummer) from the confirmation letter and write it on every later letter.

Tip: if a tax ID is still missing, ask the tax office (Finanzamt) for it or send the application with a note that it will follow. Waiting months for perfect paperwork costs money.

Child benefit and Jobcenter benefits

If your family receives Jobcenter benefits, child benefit counts as the child's income and reduces the benefits by the same amount. You still have to apply for it, because the Jobcenter calculates as if you receive it. Send the child benefit decision to the Jobcenter as soon as it arrives. If you start working and leave Jobcenter benefits, child benefit stays in full.

The child supplement (Kinderzuschlag)

The child supplement is meant for parents who earn enough for themselves but not enough for their children. It is paid in addition to child benefit by the family benefits office. If you receive it, you usually also get school support, free lunch at school or Kita, and often a reduction of Kita fees. The family benefits office offers an online check that shows whether you might be eligible. Apply for it as soon as you have a regular income, because it often makes leaving Jobcenter benefits possible.

Parental allowance (Elterngeld) after a birth

Parental allowance replaces part of the income of a parent who stays home to care for a newborn. Whether you can receive it depends on your residence permit and, in some cases, on employment. In Bavaria, applications are handled by the Bavarian Centre for Family and Social Affairs (ZBFS). Ask there early, ideally during pregnancy, because the application must be made within the first months after birth to receive the full period.

Single parents: advance maintenance payments

If you raise your child alone and the other parent does not pay maintenance, or pays too little, the youth welfare office (Jugendamt) can pay advance maintenance (Unterhaltsvorschuss). The state then tries to recover the money from the other parent. This also applies when the other parent is in Ukraine or cannot be reached. Ask the youth welfare office of your district which documents it needs in your case.

Education and participation package

Families receiving Jobcenter benefits, housing benefit or the child supplement can apply for the education and participation package. It covers school trips, a school supply allowance, lunch at school or Kita, transport to school in some cases, tutoring if the child needs it, and a monthly amount for clubs, music lessons or sports. Apply to the office that pays your main benefit. Many families miss this support simply because they never applied.

Housing benefit (Wohngeld)

Families with their own income who do not receive Jobcenter benefits may be eligible for housing benefit, a subsidy for rent paid by the town or district. It is often combined with the child supplement. The calculation depends on income, rent and household size; the housing benefit office (Wohngeldstelle) of your town can check it.

Report changes

Changes must be reported to the office that pays: a move, a child leaving school or training, a child moving out, a new job, or a longer stay abroad. If you do not report changes, overpayments must be paid back later. Keep copies of everything you send and receive.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting too long to apply and losing months that cannot be paid retroactively.
  • Not sending the child benefit decision to the Jobcenter, which leads to repayment demands.
  • Not applying for the education package because "it is only a small amount".
  • Applying for parental allowance too late after birth.
  • Using different spellings of names on different forms.

Your checklist

  1. Register your address and wait for the tax IDs of every family member.
  2. Apply for child benefit at the family benefits office straight away.
  3. Send the decision to the Jobcenter, if you receive benefits.
  4. Apply for the education and participation package for school and Kita.
  5. Once you work, check the child supplement and housing benefit.
  6. Expecting a baby? Ask the ZBFS about parental allowance during pregnancy.

Frequently asked questions

Can Ukrainians with §24 receive child benefit?

Yes. Parents with a residence permit under §24 can receive child benefit. Apply to the family benefits office.

Does child benefit reduce Jobcenter benefits?

Yes. It counts as the child's income, but you still have to apply for it.

How far back is child benefit paid?

Usually for up to six months before the month of application. Apply early.

What is the child supplement?

An extra payment for working parents whose income is not enough for their children. It also opens access to school support.

Who pays parental allowance in Bavaria?

The Bavarian Centre for Family and Social Affairs (ZBFS). Ask early, ideally during pregnancy.
