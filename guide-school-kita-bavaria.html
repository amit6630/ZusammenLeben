Registering Your Child for School or Kita in Bavaria

In Bavaria, school is compulsory for children who live here, including children from Ukraine. Kindergarten is voluntary but extremely helpful for learning German. Here is how both work in Upper Franconia.

Updated: 26 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

School is compulsory in Bavaria

Children of school age who live in Bavaria must attend school, including children from Ukraine. Compulsory schooling usually starts at the latest three months after arrival and does not depend on your residence permit being finished. School is free of charge, and families with low income can receive support for school supplies, trips and lunch through the education and participation package (Bildung und Teilhabe, BuT). Many families also keep their children in Ukrainian online lessons. That is allowed in addition, but it does not replace attending a German school.

How the Bavarian school system is built

  • Grundschule (grades 1 to 4): all children attend primary school together.
  • Mittelschule (grades 5 to 9 or 10): practical focus, leads to vocational training.
  • Realschule (grades 5 to 10): leads to the intermediate school certificate and many training paths.
  • Gymnasium (grades 5 to 12 or 13): leads to the Abitur, the university entrance qualification.
  • Berufsschule: vocational school for young people from about 16, often combined with an apprenticeship or a special integration class.

Switching between types is possible later. For a child who arrives without German, the first year is about language and settling in, not about the "final" school type.

Which school for your child?

For primary school, the school is assigned by your address (Sprengel). Contact the school directly or ask at the town hall which one is yours. For older children, the local school office (Staatliches Schulamt) of your district advises on placement. It often starts at a Mittelschule with German support. If your child was a strong student in Ukraine, mention it and bring reports; a later move to Realschule or Gymnasium is possible once German improves.

German classes and support

Many Bavarian schools first teach newcomers in a class focused on German (Deutschklasse), usually for one to two years, and then move them into regular classes. Other schools place children directly in a regular class with extra German lessons (Deutschförderung). Both paths work. What helps most is daily contact with German-speaking classmates, homework support, and hobbies such as sports clubs or music schools.

Documents to bring

  • Passport or ID of the child and of a parent
  • Registration certificate (Meldebescheinigung)
  • Residence permit or the confirmation of your application
  • Vaccination record, especially proof of measles vaccination, which is required by law for school and Kita
  • Last Ukrainian school report, if you have it, even without translation
  • Information on illnesses, allergies or special needs

The first weeks at school

Expect a lot of paperwork: forms for lunch, excursions, photo consent and emergency contacts. Ask the class teacher for a short meeting and the best way to communicate, for example email or a messenger group of the parents. School supplies are bought by parents; the school gives a list. German schools expect parents to attend parent evenings (Elternabend) and to excuse absences in writing on the same day. If your child is ill, call or write to the school in the morning.

Tip: ask whether the school has a social worker (Schulsozialarbeit). They help with integration, conflicts and family questions, and often know other Ukrainian families at the school.

Kindergarten and nursery (Kita)

From the age of one, every child in Germany has a legal right to a place in childcare. There are nurseries for children under three (Krippe), kindergartens from three until school starts (Kindergarten), and after-school care for primary pupils (Hort). Kita is voluntary, but for learning German it is one of the most effective steps a family can take.

In practice places are limited, so register early with several Kitas in your town and put your child on each waiting list. Visit them, ask about opening hours and settling-in (Eingewöhnung), which usually takes two to four weeks with a parent present at first. If no place is offered, the youth welfare office (Jugendamt) of your district can help you find one.

Fees and financial help

Kita fees depend on the town and the hours booked. In Bavaria the state supports kindergarten fees for older children, and families receiving benefits often have fees reduced or covered by the youth welfare office. Lunch can be covered through the education and participation package. Ask the town hall or the Kita leadership which forms apply to you. Child benefit (Kindergeld) is paid by the family benefits office and is a separate application.

From our experience: in smaller towns such as Helmbrechts or Naila, a place is often found faster than in Hof. Being flexible about the town can save months.

Teenagers: certificates and the path to training

For young people aged 15 to 18 the question is often not only school but the next step. A German school-leaving certificate opens the way to vocational training (Ausbildung), the dual system in which young people work in a company and attend vocational school at the same time, with a monthly training allowance. Ukrainian certificates can be evaluated, and career counselling at the employment agency (Berufsberatung) is free and also available for pupils. Internships (Praktikum) during school holidays help teenagers find out which job suits them and are often the first door to a training place.

When children struggle

War, separation from relatives and a new language weigh heavily on children. Sleep problems, withdrawal or anger in the first months are common reactions. Talk to the class teacher and the school social worker early; they can adapt expectations. Free counselling is available at family and educational counselling centres (Erziehungsberatungsstelle), and some offer conversations in Ukrainian or Russian. Regular routines, sports and friendships with classmates help more than extra lessons.

Frequently asked questions

Does my child have to go to school in Bavaria?

Yes. School is compulsory for school-age children who live in Bavaria, including children from Ukraine.

Is there a right to a Kita place?

Yes, from the age of one. Because places are limited, register early with several Kitas.

Is measles vaccination required?

Yes. Proof of measles protection is required for school and Kita in Germany.

Can my child continue Ukrainian online school?

Yes, in addition. It does not replace attending a German school, which is compulsory.

Who pays for school trips and lunch?

Families with low income can apply for the education and participation package, which covers trips, lunch and school supplies.
