Driving in Germany with a Ukrainian Licence

In rural Upper Franconia a car often decides whether you can reach a job, a school or a doctor. Here is what applies to Ukrainian driving licences and what you need before you drive.

Updated: 26 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

Why a car matters in Upper Franconia

In rural Upper Franconia a car often decides whether you can reach a job with early shifts, a school in the next town or a specialist doctor in Hof. Buses run less often in the evenings and at weekends. At the same time, owning a car in Germany comes with clear rules and costs. This guide explains what applies to Ukrainian driving licences and what you need before you drive.

Is my Ukrainian licence valid?

Under EU rules adopted in 2022, valid Ukrainian driving licences are recognised in Germany for as long as you have temporary protection. You do not need an international driving permit or a translation, and you are not required to exchange the licence while protection applies. Always carry the original licence and your residence permit when you drive, because the police may ask for both.

The licence must be valid. If it has expired, or if you only have a copy or a photo of it, you may not drive. Check the categories on your licence: they decide which vehicles you may drive, for example cars (B) or trucks (C).

When an exchange makes sense

You can exchange your licence voluntarily for a German one. This is useful if you plan to stay long term, if your Ukrainian licence will expire soon, or if you change to another residence permit later. Apply at the driving licence office (Fahrerlaubnisbehörde) of your district office. You usually need your licence, passport, residence permit, registration certificate, a biometric photo and, for some categories, an eye test and first-aid course. Ask whether theory or practical tests are required for your category. During the exchange the office keeps your Ukrainian licence, so plan the timing.

Lost or expired licence

If your licence was lost in the war or has expired, ask the driving licence office about the options before you drive. Sometimes a confirmation from the Ukrainian authorities helps; in other cases you have to take lessons and exams for a German licence. Driving without a valid licence is a criminal offence in Germany and can also affect your residence status.

Getting a German licence from scratch

If you never had a licence, you train at a driving school (Fahrschule), take a first-aid course and an eye test, and pass a theory and a practical exam. Ask driving schools which languages the theory exam is offered in, and about the total costs, which can be considerable. The Jobcenter or employment agency can sometimes support a licence if it is needed for a specific job.

Insurance and registration

  • Every car in Germany needs liability insurance (Kfz-Haftpflichtversicherung) before registration. Your insurer gives you an electronic confirmation number (eVB).
  • A car is registered at the vehicle registration office (Zulassungsstelle) of your district. You need ID, registration certificate, the eVB number, the vehicle papers and a SEPA mandate for the vehicle tax.
  • Every car needs a valid technical inspection (Hauptuntersuchung, often called TÜV). The date is shown on a sticker on the rear number plate.
  • Cars with Ukrainian plates are subject to special rules and time limits for temporary use. Check the current rules with customs or the registration office.

Buying a used car

Buy from a dealer or a private seller with a written contract. Check the inspection date, the mileage, signs of accidents and whether all papers are present. Take someone who knows cars to the viewing, and never pay in full before you receive the car and the papers. Ask your insurer for a quote before buying; insurance for new drivers in Germany can be expensive, and your Ukrainian no-claims history is not always recognised.

Rules that surprise newcomers: winter tyres are required in winter conditions, the general alcohol limit is 0.5 per mille and zero for drivers under 21 and new drivers, and phones may not be held while driving. In towns the speed limit is usually 50 km/h, outside towns 100 km/h. Speed cameras are common, also in small towns.

If something happens

After an accident, secure the scene with hazard lights and a warning triangle, help injured people and call 112 if anyone is hurt. Exchange names, addresses and insurance details with the other driver, and take photos. Call the police if there are injuries, if the other driver refuses to cooperate, or if you are unsure. Report the accident to your insurer quickly. Every car must carry a warning triangle, a first-aid kit and a high-visibility vest.

Fines and letters about traffic offences

If a speed camera photographs your car, the owner receives a letter. A hearing form (Anhörungsbogen) asks who was driving; answer within the deadline. Then a warning fine (Verwarnungsgeld) or a fine notice (Bußgeldbescheid) follows. Serious offences add points in the central register in Flensburg and can lead to a driving ban. Pay on time or object within the deadline stated in the letter; ignoring it makes it more expensive. The yearly vehicle tax (Kfz-Steuer) is collected by direct debit from customs.

Cycling and walking

Bicycles must have working brakes, lights and a bell. Cyclists use cycle paths where they exist, and the same alcohol and phone rules apply in a stricter form than many expect. Children up to eight ride on the pavement. Helmets are not compulsory, but strongly recommended. In the dark, wear bright clothes, also as a pedestrian on country roads.

No car? Regional transport

Buses and regional trains connect most towns in our area. The Deutschland-Ticket gives monthly travel on local and regional transport throughout Germany at a fixed price, and some employers subsidise it. Many towns are also comfortable by bicycle in summer. Ask the Jobcenter whether travel costs to work or courses can be reimbursed.

How we help

We help with appointments at the driving licence and registration offices, explain letters about vehicle tax and fines, and connect families with people who can check a used car. Ask for help.

Frequently asked questions

Can I drive in Germany with a Ukrainian licence?

Yes, a valid Ukrainian licence is recognised while you have temporary protection. Carry the original and your residence permit.

Do I need a translation?

No. Under the EU rules for Ukrainian licences, no translation or international permit is needed.

Where do I exchange my licence?

At the driving licence office (Fahrerlaubnisbehörde) of your district office.

What if my licence has expired?

Do not drive. Ask the driving licence office about your options first.

What must every car carry?

A warning triangle, a first-aid kit and a high-visibility vest.
