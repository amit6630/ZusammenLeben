Finding an Apartment in Upper Franconia with Jobcenter Support

Housing is the first question almost every family asks us. This guide explains the order of steps that works in Northern Bavaria, so the Jobcenter can approve your rent and you do not end up paying for a lease on your own.

Updated: 26 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

Why the order of steps matters

Most housing problems we see in Upper Franconia do not start with a lack of apartments. They start with steps taken in the wrong order: a lease signed too early, a deposit paid from savings, or a move to a town where the Jobcenter is not responsible. In Germany, authorities work strictly by procedure. If you follow the sequence below, the Jobcenter can approve your rent quickly and you keep full control over your money.

1. Clarify your situation before you search

Before you open a single listing, answer three questions. First, which Jobcenter is responsible for you? It depends on the district (Landkreis) or city where you are registered. Second, does your residence permit contain a residence condition (Wohnsitzauflage) that ties you to a district? If it does, you can only rent inside that area unless the immigration office agrees to a change. Third, how many people will live in the apartment, and how old are the children? Household size decides how many square metres and how much rent are considered appropriate.

2. Know the rent limit in writing

If the Jobcenter pays your housing costs, it covers only rent that is "appropriate" (angemessen) for your town and household size. Each district sets its own limits, so the figures in Hof, Wunsiedel and Kulmbach are not the same. The limits usually separate the cold rent (Kaltmiete), the service charges (Nebenkosten) and the heating costs (Heizkosten). Ask your caseworker to give you the current limits in writing, or by email.

Tip: keep a photo of the limits on your phone. When a landlord asks "Will the Jobcenter pay?", you can answer on the spot.

3. Where to look in Upper Franconia

  • Online portals: ImmobilienScout24, Immowelt and Kleinanzeigen. Filter by town, rent and number of rooms, and save the search so you receive new offers by email.
  • Housing companies and cooperatives: municipal housing companies (Wohnungsbaugesellschaften) and cooperatives (Wohnungsgenossenschaften) in Hof, Selb, Münchberg, Helmbrechts and Naila often have waiting lists. Register early, even if nothing is free today.
  • Local notices: supermarkets, churches, town halls and local newspapers. In small towns many apartments are never posted online.
  • Personal network: neighbours, colleagues, the school and the Ukrainian community often hear about free apartments first.
  • Our network: some apartments are managed directly within the ZusammenLeben initiative, and we know landlords who already work with the Jobcenter. See our services.

4. Contacting landlords

Landlords in our region receive many enquiries. A short, polite message in German helps you stand out. Mention who will live in the apartment, that the Jobcenter will cover the rent within the local limits, when you could move in, and a phone number. If you do not yet speak German well, write that someone can help with communication. Many landlords do not know the Jobcenter process; we can explain it to them.

Go to viewings on time, bring your ID and residence permit, and check the apartment carefully: heating, windows, signs of mould, and whether a kitchen is included. In Germany many apartments are rented without a kitchen.

5. Get a written rental offer (Mietangebot)

When a landlord agrees, ask for a written rental offer. It should contain the address, size in square metres, number of rooms, cold rent, service charges, heating costs, deposit and the planned moving-in date. Submit this offer to the Jobcenter together with the form for a change of address or the application for approval of housing costs (Zusicherung zu den Aufwendungen für die neue Unterkunft). Then wait for the written answer.

Golden rule: never sign the lease before the Jobcenter has approved the offer in writing. If you sign first and the rent is above the limit, you may have to pay the difference yourself every month.

6. Deposit, furniture and moving costs

The deposit (Kaution) can be up to three months' cold rent. The Jobcenter usually grants it as a loan that is repaid in small monthly amounts from your benefits. For a first own home you can apply for an initial furnishing allowance (Erstausstattung), which covers basic furniture and household items. Moving costs, such as a rented van, can also be covered if the move was approved. Apply for all of this before you buy or book anything, and keep every receipt.

7. Signing the lease

Read the lease (Mietvertrag) before signing, ideally with someone who speaks German well. Pay attention to the notice period, the rules on renovation when you move out, the payment date of the rent, and who pays which service charges. Check that the figures match the offer the Jobcenter approved. At the handover, the landlord and you sign a handover record (Übergabeprotokoll) that lists existing damage and meter readings. Take photos of every room and of the meters.

8. After moving in: registration and notifications

Your landlord must give you a confirmation of moving in (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung). With it, register your new address at the citizens' office (Bürgerbüro) within two weeks. Then send the registration certificate to:

  • the Jobcenter, so that rent payments go to the new address;
  • your health insurance fund and your bank;
  • the family benefits office (Familienkasse), if you receive child benefit;
  • schools and kindergartens, if your children change institutions.

Register electricity in your name with a supplier and keep the first meter reading. The broadcasting fee office will contact you; if you receive benefits, you can apply for an exemption.

9. Living as a tenant in Germany

A few rules make life with landlords and neighbours easier: quiet hours (Ruhezeiten) are usually at night and on Sundays, waste is separated into several bins, and apartments must be aired several times a day to prevent mould, especially in winter. If something breaks, inform the landlord in writing. Once a year you receive a statement of service charges (Nebenkostenabrechnung). Send it to the Jobcenter at once, because back payments can often be covered and credits must be reported.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sign a lease before the Jobcenter approves it?

You can, but you carry the risk. If the Jobcenter later finds the rent too high, it may only pay the appropriate part. Always get written approval first.

Who pays the deposit?

In most cases the Jobcenter grants the deposit as a loan, repaid in small monthly instalments from your benefits.

How fast must I register my new address?

Within two weeks of moving in, at the citizens' office of your town, using the landlord's confirmation.

Can I move to another district?

Only if your residence permit has no residence condition, or if the immigration office approves the change. Ask before you search in another district.

What if the apartment has no kitchen?

That is common in Germany. Ask the Jobcenter about the initial furnishing allowance before buying, or ask us about donated kitchen equipment.
