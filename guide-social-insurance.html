Social Insurance in Germany Explained

A large part of the deductions on a German pay slip goes into social insurance. It protects you and your family against illness, unemployment, accidents, old age and the need for care. Knowing how it works helps you understand your rights.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

The five branches

German social insurance has five branches: health insurance, long-term care insurance, pension insurance, unemployment insurance and accident insurance. Almost every employee is a compulsory member of all five. Contributions are a percentage of gross pay and are shared between employer and employee; accident insurance is paid by the employer alone. Your pay slip shows each contribution separately.

Your social insurance number

When you start your first job subject to social insurance, the pension insurance automatically assigns you a social insurance number (Sozialversicherungsnummer) and sends it by post. Your employer needs it. It stays the same for your whole working life. If you have not received it, your health insurance fund can request it for you.

Health insurance

Health insurance pays for doctors, hospitals, medicines and much more. As an employee you choose a statutory fund and are insured through your job. Children and a spouse without own income are usually insured free of charge through family insurance. If you are ill for a longer time, you receive your wage from the employer for up to six weeks, then sickness benefit (Krankengeld) from the fund. See our guide to health insurance for details.

Long-term care insurance

Care insurance helps when someone needs long-term help in daily life because of age, illness or disability. After an assessment, the person receives a care level (Pflegegrad) from 1 to 5, which determines support: money for care by relatives, professional care services at home, day care or a care home contribution. You apply through your health insurance fund, which also runs the care fund.

Pension insurance

Pension contributions build your entitlement to a state pension in old age, and also to disability pensions if you can no longer work for health reasons, and survivors' pensions for family members. A general minimum of five years of contributions is needed for a regular old-age pension. From a certain age and contribution period, you receive an annual pension information letter (Renteninformation) showing your expected pension. Ask the German pension insurance whether periods worked in Ukraine can be taken into account in your case.

Unemployment insurance

If you lose your job after paying contributions for at least twelve months within the last thirty months, you can receive unemployment benefit (Arbeitslosengeld, ALG I) from the employment agency. It is a percentage of your previous net pay and is paid for a limited time, depending on how long you worked and your age. Register as a job seeker with the employment agency as soon as you know your job will end, at the latest three days after learning about it.

Important: unemployment benefit from the employment agency is different from Jobcenter benefits. ALG I depends on previous work and contributions; Jobcenter benefits depend on need. Both can be combined if ALG I is very low.

Accident insurance

Statutory accident insurance covers accidents at work, on the direct way to and from work, and occupational diseases. Children are also insured at Kita, school and on the way there. It pays treatment, rehabilitation and, if needed, a pension. After an accident at work, tell your employer and the doctor that it was a work accident, so the right insurance pays.

Minijobs and social insurance

In a minijob you pay no health or unemployment insurance contributions, and you are not insured through the job for health. You are, however, compulsorily insured in the pension insurance unless you apply for an exemption. Staying in the pension insurance costs little and gives you entitlements, such as counting periods for rehabilitation. Accident insurance applies in minijobs too.

Social insurance for self-employed people

Self-employed people are generally not compulsorily insured in pension and unemployment insurance, with exceptions for some professions. They must insure themselves for health and care. Voluntary unemployment insurance is possible under certain conditions, and voluntary pension contributions too. See our guide to self-employment.

Social insurance and Jobcenter benefits

If you receive Jobcenter benefits, the Jobcenter pays your health and care insurance contributions. When you start work, your employer takes over. If you work in a regular job and still receive top-up benefits, you are insured through the job. Report every change to your health insurance fund and the Jobcenter.

Reading the deductions on your pay slip

  • KV: health insurance (Krankenversicherung)
  • PV: long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung); childless employees from a certain age pay a small extra
  • RV: pension insurance (Rentenversicherung)
  • AV: unemployment insurance (Arbeitslosenversicherung)

Together these are often around a fifth of gross pay for the employee. The employer pays a similar amount on top, which does not appear as a deduction.

When you are ill: the procedure

If you are ill and cannot work, inform your employer on the first day, before your shift starts. From a certain day, often the fourth, you need a doctor's certificate of incapacity for work; some employers want it from the first day. The doctor now usually reports it electronically to your health insurance fund, and your employer retrieves it there, but you must still tell the employer yourself that you are ill and for how long. During illness you are protected: you keep your wage for up to six weeks per illness.

Rehabilitation

After a serious illness or accident, the pension or accident insurance can pay for medical rehabilitation (Reha), for example several weeks in a specialised clinic, so that you can return to work. Professional rehabilitation, such as retraining for a new job when your health no longer allows the old one, is also possible. Your doctor applies with you.

Changing jobs and gaps

When you change jobs, your insurances continue automatically with the new employer; tell the new employer your health insurance fund and social insurance number. If there is a gap between two jobs, register with the employment agency or Jobcenter straight away so that you remain insured. Never leave a job without clarifying how you will be insured afterwards.

Your rights if something goes wrong

Decisions of insurance funds can be challenged with an objection (Widerspruch), usually within one month, just like Jobcenter decisions. Social courts are free for insured people. Independent advice is available from consumer advice centres, social associations and migration counselling.

Frequently asked questions

Which insurances are deducted from my wage?

Health, long-term care, pension and unemployment insurance. Accident insurance is paid by the employer.

When can I receive unemployment benefit?

After at least twelve months of contributions within the last thirty months.

Am I insured in a minijob?

Not for health through the job, but in pension and accident insurance, unless you opt out of pension insurance.

Who pays after a work accident?

The statutory accident insurance. Tell the doctor and employer it was a work accident.

How do I apply for care support?

Through your health insurance fund, which runs the care fund and arranges an assessment.
