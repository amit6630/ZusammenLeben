Getting Your Ukrainian Qualifications Recognised in Germany

Many of the parents we support are nurses, teachers, engineers or accountants. Recognition of a Ukrainian diploma is often the key to working in your own profession instead of starting from zero.

Updated: 26 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

Why recognition matters

Many of the parents we support were nurses, teachers, engineers, accountants, electricians or doctors in Ukraine. In Germany, the value of that experience depends a lot on paperwork. Recognition (Anerkennung) is an official comparison of your Ukrainian qualification with the German reference profession. It is often the key to working in your own profession, earning a fair wage and building a long-term future here, instead of starting from zero in an unskilled job.

Regulated or not regulated?

The first question is whether your profession is regulated in Germany.

  • Regulated professions may only be practised with official recognition. Examples: doctor, dentist, pharmacist, nurse, midwife, physiotherapist, teacher at a state school, educator in a Kita, lawyer, and some engineering titles. Without recognition you may not work in these professions, or only in assisting roles.
  • Non-regulated professions, such as most jobs in IT, business, administration, logistics and many technical fields, have no legal requirement. You can apply directly. A recognition statement is still useful, because it shows employers what your diploma is worth in German terms.
  • Academic degrees that do not lead to a regulated profession can be evaluated by the central office for foreign education (Zentralstelle für ausländisches Bildungswesen, ZAB). Its statement of comparability helps with job applications and further studies.

Find the right authority

Responsibility depends on the profession and the federal state. The official "Recognition Finder" on the federal portal anerkennung-in-deutschland.de asks for your profession and the place where you want to work, then shows the responsible office in Bavaria, the documents it requires and the fees. In Bavaria, for example, health professions, teaching, crafts and industrial trades are handled by different offices. Crafts are usually assessed by the chamber of crafts (Handwerkskammer), industrial and commercial trades by the chamber of industry and commerce (IHK FOSA).

Documents you usually need

  • Diploma and the supplement listing subjects and hours (dodatok)
  • Proof of work experience, for example the employment record book (trudova knyzhka) or employer references
  • Passport and registration certificate
  • CV in table form, in German
  • Certified German translations by a sworn translator (beeidigte Übersetzung)
  • For regulated professions sometimes: proof of German language level, a medical certificate and a certificate of good conduct

Ask the responsible office which documents must be certified copies and which can be simple copies. Never send originals. If documents were lost in the war, say so: in many cases a skills analysis (Qualifikationsanalyse) can replace missing papers, where you show your skills in a practical test.

Costs and who pays them

Recognition procedures cost fees, and certified translations add more. The Jobcenter or the employment agency can often cover these costs if recognition improves your chances of finding work. Apply for coverage before you submit, because costs already paid are often not refunded. There is also a federal recognition grant (Anerkennungszuschuss) for people who cannot get support elsewhere. Ask your caseworker and keep every receipt.

Tip: book free recognition counselling first. Advisers of the IQ Network help you choose the right reference profession, check your documents and plan the costs. A good choice of reference profession can decide between full and partial recognition.

Your application step by step

  1. Find your German reference profession and the responsible office with the Recognition Finder.
  2. Book a free counselling appointment and bring copies of all your documents.
  3. Ask the Jobcenter or employment agency in writing to cover fees and translations.
  4. Order certified translations only of the documents the office actually requires.
  5. Submit the application with the form of the responsible office, and keep a complete copy.
  6. Answer requests for missing documents quickly; the deadline only starts once the file is complete.
  7. After the decision, plan the next step with your adviser: job search, adaptation course or exam.

How long it takes and what the result means

Once your file is complete, the office usually decides within a few months. The result is one of three outcomes:

  • Full equivalence (volle Gleichwertigkeit): your qualification counts like the German one.
  • Partial equivalence (teilweise Gleichwertigkeit): the decision lists the differences. An adaptation course (Anpassungsqualifizierung) or an exam closes the gap.
  • No equivalence: the differences are too large. Even then, the decision explains what is missing, and further training can lead to a German qualification.

Partial recognition is normal and is not a failure. Many adaptation courses can be completed while working, and for regulated health professions they often take place in a hospital or care facility.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Paying for translations before asking the Jobcenter to cover them.
  • Choosing a reference profession that is too narrow, which leads to partial recognition only.
  • Sending originals by post instead of certified copies.
  • Waiting for recognition before starting a German course.
  • Ignoring a request for missing documents, which pauses the whole procedure.

Language is part of recognition

For many regulated professions you also need German at a certain level, usually B1 or B2, and for doctors additional medical German. Start the integration course or a job-related language course (Berufssprachkurs) in parallel with recognition. Waiting until recognition is finished costs valuable months.

Working while you wait

In many fields you can already work during the procedure: as a nursing assistant, a teaching assistant, in a related role or as a trainee. This gives you income, German practice and German references, which also help the procedure. Ask the employer and the responsible office what is allowed in your case.

Teachers and educators from Ukraine

Teachers are often needed in Bavaria, especially for German classes with Ukrainian children. Full recognition as a state school teacher usually requires two subjects and good German, but many schools hire Ukrainian teachers as teaching assistants or for supplementary lessons in the meantime. Educators and nursery teachers can apply for recognition as a state-recognised educator (Erzieherin) or start as a childcare worker.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need recognition to work in Germany?

Only for regulated professions such as doctor, nurse or teacher. For most other jobs you can apply directly.

Who pays for recognition?

Often the Jobcenter or employment agency covers costs if recognition improves your job prospects. Ask before applying.

What does partial recognition mean?

Parts of your training are missing compared with the German standard. An adaptation course or exam closes the gap.

My documents were lost. What now?

Tell the responsible office. A skills analysis with a practical test can often replace missing documents.

Can I work while the procedure runs?

In many fields yes, for example as an assistant or in a related role. Ask the employer and the office what is allowed.
