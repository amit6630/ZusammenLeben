The Integration Course: Your Fastest Route to German

The integration course is the main state-funded German course for newcomers. It combines language lessons with an orientation module about life, law and society in Germany.

Updated: 26 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

What the integration course is

The integration course (Integrationskurs) is the main state-funded German course for newcomers. It is organised by the Federal Office for Migration and Refugees (BAMF) and run by approved course providers such as adult education centres (Volkshochschule) and private language schools. For most adults from Ukraine it is the fastest structured way to reach everyday German, and its certificate is recognised by employers and authorities.

What the course includes

  • A language course of usually 600 lessons in several modules, leading from beginner level to B1.
  • An orientation course of about 100 lessons on the German legal system, history, culture, democracy and everyday life.
  • Lessons usually take place on weekdays, mornings or afternoons, about four to five lessons a day. Some providers also offer evening or online courses.

Special course formats

Not everyone learns the same way, so there are special formats:

  • Parent and women's courses with topics about school and education, often with childcare.
  • Youth courses for young adults up to 27 who want to continue to school or training.
  • Literacy courses for people who are not yet confident reading and writing in the Latin alphabet.
  • Intensive courses with fewer lessons for fast learners who already study well.
  • Online courses combined with classroom days, useful for people who work shifts.

How to get a place

People with temporary protection from Ukraine can be admitted to the integration course. There are two common routes. If you receive Jobcenter benefits, your caseworker can oblige or allow you to attend; the confirmation then comes through the Jobcenter and the course is free for you. If you do not receive benefits, you apply to BAMF for admission. With the admission (Berechtigungsschein or Zulassung), you choose a course provider near you, take a placement test and receive a start date.

In Upper Franconia, course providers in Hof, Selb, Münchberg, Marktredwitz and other towns offer integration courses. Waiting times vary. Ask several providers about the next start and the level of the group.

Costs

For people who receive Jobcenter benefits, the course is free. Others usually pay a reduced contribution per lesson, and can apply to BAMF for an exemption if their income is low. If you pass the final exam within a set time, part of the contribution can be refunded. Travel costs to the course can be reimbursed in some cases; ask BAMF or the Jobcenter before the course starts.

The placement test and your level

Before the course, the provider tests what you already know. Do not worry about the result: it only decides in which module you start. If you already speak some German, you may skip the first modules and finish sooner. Be honest in the test; starting too high makes the course harder, not faster.

What a course day looks like

A typical group has 15 to 25 adults from many countries, so German is the only common language from the first day. Lessons combine grammar, vocabulary and a lot of practice for real situations: talking to a doctor, filling in forms, writing an email to a school, calling a landlord, reading a letter from an authority. Homework is part of the course. Plan about one hour a day at home for repetition; it makes a clear difference at the exam. Most teachers welcome questions, also after class.

Preparing for the exam

The DTZ tests listening, reading, writing and speaking. In the speaking part you talk with a partner about everyday topics and plan something together, for example a party or a trip. Practise this with classmates. For "Life in Germany", BAMF publishes the full catalogue of questions online, and you can practise them in a free test centre app. Ask the course provider for mock exams in the last weeks.

The final exams

At the end you take the "German Test for Immigrants" (Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer, DTZ), which shows whether you reached A2 or B1, and the test "Life in Germany" (Leben in Deutschland) on the orientation part. Passing both gives you the integration course certificate (Zertifikat Integrationskurs). It helps with job applications, longer residence permits and, later, naturalisation. If you do not reach B1 the first time, you can usually repeat part of the course once.

From our experience: families who start the course in the first months progress fastest at work and with authorities. Do not wait until housing, school and everything else are settled; the course itself makes those steps easier.

Childcare and family life during the course

For parents of small children, childcare is often the biggest obstacle. Ask the course provider whether it offers a parent course with childcare, and register your child for a Kita early, because a Kita place makes regular attendance much easier. If both parents need the course, ask whether one can attend in the morning and the other in the afternoon. Explain your situation to the Jobcenter; it can take childcare into account when planning your course.

Attendance rules

Regular attendance is required. If the Jobcenter obliged you to attend, missing lessons without a reason can lead to a reduction of benefits. If you are ill, inform the course provider and send a doctor's certificate. Keep a copy of your attendance confirmation; the Jobcenter may ask for it.

After the integration course

For many jobs and recognition procedures, B1 is not enough. After the integration course you can continue with job-related German courses (Berufssprachkurse) up to B2 or C1, also funded by BAMF. Some focus on specific fields such as health care, technology or retail. Ask your Jobcenter or the employment agency, which can give you an admission.

Learning faster outside the classroom

  • Speak German every day, even a little: with neighbours, at the bakery, with your children's teachers.
  • Join a language café or a volunteer conversation group; many towns in our region have one.
  • Use free online tools from Deutsche Welle and the apps your course recommends.
  • Watch children's programmes and the news in easy German.
  • Join a club: sports, choir or fire brigade. It brings friends and practice at the same time.

If the course is full

Use the waiting time actively with free online courses, conversation cafés and volunteer groups. On our page Learning German we collect options in the region, and we can help you find a provider with a free place.

Frequently asked questions

Is the integration course free?

For people who receive Jobcenter benefits it is free. Others pay a reduced contribution or apply for an exemption at BAMF.

What level do I reach?

The course aims at level B1. The final exam shows whether you reached A2 or B1.

Can I attend if I have small children?

Yes. There are parent courses with childcare. Ask the course provider.

Can I work and attend the course?

Yes. Many providers offer evening, part-time or online formats that can be combined with a job.

What comes after B1?

Job-related German courses up to B2 or C1, also funded by BAMF. Ask the Jobcenter or employment agency for admission.
