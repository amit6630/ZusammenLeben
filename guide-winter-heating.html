Getting Through the German Winter

Winters in Upper Franconia are cold, dark and often snowy, with frost from November to March. Heating correctly keeps your flat healthy and your bills under control. This guide explains heating, ventilation, energy bills and help when costs get too high.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

How heating works in German flats

Most flats have radiators with a thermostat valve numbered from 1 to 5. The numbers are not power levels but target temperatures: level 3 means about 20 °C. Turning the valve to 5 does not heat the room faster; it only heats it longer and wastes energy. Central heating for the whole building is usually run by the landlord, and you pay your share in the service charges. Some flats have their own gas boiler or night storage heaters; ask the landlord how your heating works.

The right temperature

  • Living room: about 20 °C.
  • Kitchen: about 18 °C.
  • Bedroom: 16 to 18 °C.
  • Bathroom: about 22 °C when in use.

Each degree less saves around six percent of heating energy. Do not turn the heating off completely in winter: cold walls attract moisture and mould, and heating a cold flat back up costs more. At night and when you are away, reduce by a few degrees only.

Ventilation: short and wide open

German flats are well insulated, so moisture from cooking, showering, drying clothes and breathing stays inside. The solution is shock ventilation (Stoßlüften): open the windows wide for five to ten minutes, two to four times a day, ideally with a cross draught. Turn the radiator valves down while you ventilate. Windows tilted open for hours cool the walls and waste heating without removing much moisture.

Rule of thumb: ventilate after showering, cooking and in the morning after sleeping. A cheap hygrometer shows the humidity: in winter it should stay between 40 and 60 percent.

Preventing mould

Mould appears as black or green spots, often in corners, behind furniture and around windows. To prevent it, keep furniture a few centimetres away from outside walls, dry laundry outside or in a ventilated room if possible, and wipe condensation off windows in the morning. If mould appears, inform the landlord in writing and take photos. The landlord must remove mould caused by building defects. Small spots on smooth surfaces can be cleaned with alcohol-based products; large areas belong to professionals, especially if children or people with asthma live in the flat.

Understanding energy bills

You usually pay monthly advance payments (Abschläge) for electricity and, if you have your own contract, gas. Once a year you receive a final bill: if you used more, you pay extra; if you used less, you get money back. Heating costs for central heating come with the annual service charge statement (Nebenkostenabrechnung). Read meters yourself at the start and end of a contract and take photos. If the annual bill shows a large back payment, you can ask for higher monthly payments to avoid a shock next year.

Saving electricity

  • Switch off devices completely instead of leaving them on standby; use power strips with a switch.
  • Use LED bulbs.
  • Cook with a lid, boil water in a kettle and defrost the freezer regularly.
  • Wash clothes at 30 or 40 degrees and use full loads.
  • Avoid electric fan heaters: they are very expensive to run.

Comparing providers

If you have your own electricity or gas contract, compare prices once a year. The basic supply tariff (Grundversorgung) of the local utility is often more expensive than other tariffs. Check the contract duration, notice period and price guarantee before switching, and beware of doorstep sellers. The consumer advice centres offer free or cheap independent energy advice, also at home.

Help with high energy costs

If you receive Jobcenter or social welfare benefits, the authority pays reasonable heating costs in addition to the rent; send them the annual bill. Electricity, however, is part of the standard benefit. If a large back payment arrives, ask the Jobcenter immediately, because heating back payments can be covered. If you have income but cannot pay, contact the provider early and ask for instalments. Electricity can only be cut off after reminders and a warning; debt counselling and social services can often prevent it.

Housing allowance

People with low income who do not receive Jobcenter benefits may receive housing allowance (Wohngeld), which includes a heating cost component. Apply at the town or district office; see our guide to finding a flat and guide to saving money.

Warm clothing on a small budget

Good winter jackets, boots, hats and gloves do not have to be new. Clothing stores of the Red Cross, Caritas and Diakonie, second-hand shops and online marketplaces offer cheap or free winter clothes. Children grow quickly, so swapping with other families makes sense. Layered clothing at home also lets you keep the heating a little lower.

Snow, ice and your duties

In many buildings, tenants must clear snow and spread grit on the pavement in front of the house in rotation (Winterdienst), usually early in the morning on working days. Check your tenancy agreement or the house rules. If someone slips because the path was not cleared, the responsible person can be liable. On the road, winter tyres are mandatory in wintry conditions; see our guide to driving.

Health in the dark season

Short days can affect mood and sleep. Go outside in daylight every day, even when it is cloudy, keep regular sleep times and move your body. Ask your doctor about vitamin D if you feel constantly tired. If low mood lasts longer, read our guide to mental health. The flu and COVID vaccinations are recommended for older people and people with chronic illnesses.

Safety in winter

Never use a gas cooker, barbecue or open flame to heat a room: the risk of carbon monoxide poisoning and fire is high. Do not cover radiators or electric heaters with clothes. Candles are popular in the German winter but should never burn unattended. Check that smoke detectors work; they are mandatory in bedrooms and hallways in Bavaria.

How we help

We explain energy bills and service charge statements, help you write to the landlord about mould or a broken heating system, apply for heating cost support at the Jobcenter or for housing allowance, and find cheap winter clothing. Ask for help.

Frequently asked questions

What does level 3 on the radiator mean?

About 20 °C. The numbers are target temperatures, not power levels; level 5 does not heat faster.

How should I ventilate in winter?

Open windows wide for five to ten minutes, two to four times a day, instead of tilting them for hours.

Who pays for mould removal?

The landlord, if building defects cause it. Report mould in writing with photos.

Does the Jobcenter pay heating costs?

Yes, reasonable heating costs are paid in addition to rent, including back payments. Electricity is part of the standard benefit.

Do I have to clear snow?

Often yes, in rotation with other tenants. Check your tenancy agreement or house rules.

Our volunteers answer in Ukrainian, Russian, German and English. Free of charge.
