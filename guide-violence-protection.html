Protection from Violence: Where to Get Help

Violence is never private and never your fault. In Germany, the police, courts and many counselling services protect people affected by violence, whatever their residence status. Help is free, confidential and available in many languages, including Ukrainian and Russian.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

In acute danger call 110 (police) or 112 (ambulance). The violence against women helpline 116 016 is free, anonymous and open day and night, with interpreters in many languages. See also our emergency numbers.

What counts as violence

Violence is not only hitting. It includes threats, humiliation, control over money or phone, locking someone in, sexual violence, stalking and violence on the internet, for example sharing private images. Forced marriage and violence in the name of honour are also crimes. Violence can come from a partner, a family member, a host, an employer or a stranger. Men and boys can also be affected.

Domestic violence: your rights

In Germany, violence in the family is a crime and the police act on it. If the police are called, they can order the violent person to leave the flat and not return for several days, even if that person is the tenant. During this time you can apply at the family court for a protection order under the Violence Protection Act (Gewaltschutzgesetz). The court can forbid the person to approach you or contact you, and can assign the shared flat to you for a period. Urgent orders are often issued within days.

Women's shelters

Women's shelters (Frauenhäuser) offer a safe, secret address for women and their children who need to leave home. Staff support with authorities, money, school for the children and the next steps. Many shelters accept women regardless of residence status and income; costs are usually covered by the Jobcenter or social welfare office. If a shelter is full, the helpline 116 016 or the police help find a free place, also in another town. Some regions also have shelters or protected flats for men.

Counselling without leaving home

Not everyone wants to leave immediately. Counselling centres for women, intervention centres and family counselling offer conversations about your situation, safety plans and options, without obligation. You decide what happens. Counselling is confidential: the staff do not report to the police without your consent, except in cases of serious danger to children.

Making a safety plan

  • Save emergency numbers in your phone under a neutral name.
  • Keep copies of passports, residence documents, birth certificates and bank cards in a safe place outside the home or digitally.
  • Agree on a code word with a trusted person that means "call the police".
  • Pack a small bag with medicines, money, a charger and important papers.
  • Think about safe routes and places where you can go quickly.

Documenting violence

Evidence helps in court and with authorities. See a doctor after injuries and ask for a written report. Some hospitals offer confidential forensic documentation of injuries (vertrauliche Spurensicherung) without immediately involving the police, so you can decide later whether to report. Keep threatening messages, photos of injuries and damaged things, and write down dates and what happened.

Children affected by violence

Children suffer when they experience violence, also when they only witness it. The youth welfare office (Jugendamt) must protect children and can take them into care in an emergency; it also supports parents who want to protect their children. Children and teenagers can call the children's helpline 116 111 (Nummer gegen Kummer) free and anonymously. For sexual abuse, the helpline 0800 22 55 530 advises children, parents and professionals.

Sexual violence

After a rape or sexual assault, seek medical help as soon as possible, ideally within a few days, also for emergency contraception and HIV prevention. You do not have to report to the police to receive medical care. Specialist counselling centres for sexual violence support you, accompany you to the police or court if you want, and help with trauma. The emergency contraceptive pill is available in pharmacies without prescription.

Exploitation and human trafficking

Offers of work or housing that sound too good, employers who keep your passport, debts you have to "work off" or forced sex work are signs of exploitation. Specialist counselling centres for victims of human trafficking help anonymously and free of charge, also with residence questions. Victims have special rights, including a reflection period and protection during criminal proceedings.

Victims of crime

If you are a victim of a crime, such as an attack, robbery or hate crime, you can report it at any police station or online. Victim support organisations like Weisser Ring (victim helpline 116 006) help free of charge, for example with a first lawyer consultation or accompaniment to court. Victims of violent crimes can receive compensation and therapy under the social compensation law (Soziales Entschädigungsrecht).

Residence status and violence

Many women fear losing their residence if they leave a violent partner. People with temporary protection have their own residence permit, independent of the partner. For other permits linked to marriage, there are special rules for victims of violence. Get advice early at a migration counselling service or a specialist lawyer before you change your address or status.

If you use violence yourself

Stress, war experiences and isolation can lead to aggression. If you feel that you are hurting people close to you or fear that you might, help is available: perpetrator programmes and counselling for men work confidentially. Taking responsibility early protects your family and you.

Stalking and violence online

Repeated unwanted calls, messages, following you or threats on social media are stalking, which is a crime in Germany. Do not answer, but save everything: screenshots with date and time, call lists and names of witnesses. Change passwords, check which apps share your location and turn off location sharing on your phone. Platforms must delete illegal content after a report. Specialist services such as HateAid support people affected by digital violence, and the police can take reports online.

Help for helpers

If a friend, neighbour or colleague tells you about violence, listen calmly and believe them. Do not push them to act, but share the helpline number 116 016, which also advises friends and relatives. In acute danger, call 110 yourself.

How we help

We listen without judging, help you contact the helpline, counselling centres or a women's shelter, accompany you to authorities and translate. Everything you tell us stays confidential. In acute danger call 110. Ask for help.

Frequently asked questions

Who can I call at night if I am in danger?

Call the police on 110. The helpline 116 016 is free, anonymous and open 24 hours, with interpreters.

Can the police make my partner leave the flat?

Yes. The police can order a violent person to leave the flat for several days, even if that person is the tenant.

Does a women's shelter cost money?

Usually not for you: costs are mostly covered by the Jobcenter or social welfare office.

Will I lose my residence if I leave my partner?

People with temporary protection have their own permit. For other permits, get advice early; special rules protect victims.

Where can children get help?

Children and teenagers can call 116 111 free and anonymously. The youth welfare office protects children in danger.

Need personal help?

Our volunteers answer in Ukrainian, Russian, German and English. Free of charge.
