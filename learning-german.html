OFFICIAL GUIDE

Learning German: Social Integration

Levels, Certificates, Exams, and Registration — The Complete Guide.

Why Do You Need a Certificate?

In Germany, simply stating "I speak German" is not an argument an authority accepts. In many cases, official proof in the form of a certificate from a recognized institution is mandatory. You will need it primarily for:

Permanent Residence (Niederlassungserlaubnis)

In most cases, proof of German at the B1 level is required, alongside a "Leben in Deutschland" exam or orientation course.

German Citizenship (Einbürgerung)

Applicants under Section 10 of the Citizenship Act must prove B1 level German. Note: The citizenship test checks knowledge, not language skills.

Work and Studies

Many employers, vocational schools, and universities require a certificate. Recognizing qualifications acquired in Ukraine often requires a specific language level.

Integration Course Completion

The certificate is the official document proving you have successfully completed the course.

The Six Levels (CEFR)

All certificates in Germany are based on a uniform European six-level scale. The critical level is B1 — the threshold for almost all significant rights (permanent residence, citizenship, professional recognition).

A1

Beginner

Basic phrases, introducing yourself, asking and answering very simple questions.

A2

Basic

Understanding common everyday expressions, handling simple routines, understanding short texts like signs or ads.

B1

Independent (Target Level)

Understanding main points regarding work and leisure, handling most everyday situations, describing experiences.

B2

Upper Independent

Conducting professional conversations, understanding complex texts, working in most standard workplaces.

C1

Proficient

Academic studies, senior professional roles, fluent and flexible language use.

C2

Mastery

Level close to a native speaker.

Which Certificate is Right for You?

1. DTZ (Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer)

Best for: Integration course participants.

The standard integration course final exam. It provides a safety net: results range between A2 and B1. Even if you don't reach B1 in all areas, you may still walk away with an A2 certificate.

Cost: Free for course participants (usually 100-170 EUR otherwise)

2. telc Deutsch

Best for: Independent learners needing a recognized B1.

Open to everyone without requiring an integration course. Offers more exam dates and locations. telc C1 Hochschule is accepted by almost all German universities.

Cost: ~120-250 EUR depending on level and center

3. Goethe-Zertifikat

Best for: International recognition and flexibility.

The most globally recognized certificate. Modular structure allows you to take and retake individual skills separately (Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking).

Cost: Varies by module/level

4. ÖSD

Best for: Valid alternative.

An Austrian certificate that is fully recognized in Germany, including for citizenship at the B1 level. Less common in certain regions but completely valid.

Registration: Step-by-Step

1

Decide on the certificate

If in an integration course, you will take the DTZ. Otherwise, choose telc or Goethe.

2

Find a test center

Start with the local VHS (Volkshochschule). It is usually the most accessible and affordable option.

3

Register early

DTZ registration closes at least 4 weeks prior. Goethe exams close 3-4 weeks ahead. No cancellations are allowed after the deadline.

4

Prepare documents

ID or Passport. For DTZ, bring your eligibility/obligation document from BAMF, Jobcenter, or Immigration Office.

5

Pay the fees

Usually via bank transfer by a specific deadline.

Crucial Note: Registering for a preparation course is NOT a registration for the exam itself. Exams require separate registration.

The Integration Course (Funded Path)

The course includes 700 hours (600 for language up to B1, 100 for orientation regarding German culture and law).

  • Costs: 2.29 EUR per hour (~1,600 EUR total). If receiving government benefits, it is usually 100% free (BAMF covers it).
  • Refunds: If you pass within 2 years, you can claim back 50% of your paid fees.
  • Exemption applications: Must be submitted *before* starting. Retroactive exemptions are highly restricted.

⚠️ CRITICAL UPDATE: 2026 Status for Ukrainian Refugees

The situation changed twice this year. Always verify the current status.

In February 2026, BAMF paused voluntary participation approvals under Section 44(4). However, as of August 1, 2026, a new government directive reinstates voluntary integration course access for Ukrainian war refugees and others with labor market prospects.

Action: If you were rejected earlier this year, try again! Contact the Jobcenter or BAMF immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to reach B1?

A full integration course takes about 6 to 12 months. Intensive study outside class accelerates this.

I failed. What now?

For Goethe (B1+), you only retake the failed module. For DTZ via integration courses, you usually get one free retake.

Is my 2023 certificate still valid?

Generally, language certificates do not expire, but some citizenship offices prefer certificates under 5-10 years old. Check locally.

Can I take the exam online?

No. Standardized exams (Goethe, telc, DTZ) must be taken in person at a certified center. Purely commercial online tests are usually not accepted.

Are there alternatives to the exam?

Yes. Completing a German school (Hauptschule minimum) or a recognized vocational training (Ausbildung) in Germany replaces the B1 requirement.

Is there childcare during the course?

Yes, the "Integration Course with Child Plus" program runs until the end of 2026, offering free childcare. Search via BAMF-NAvI.
