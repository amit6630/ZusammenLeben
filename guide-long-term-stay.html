Planning a Long-Term Future in Germany

Temporary protection for people from Ukraine currently runs until 4 March 2027. Many families now ask what comes next. Nobody knows yet exactly how the rules will look after that date, but you can prepare today, so that you have options whatever is decided.

Updated: 28 October 20265 min readZusammenLeben

Where things stand

Residence permits under section 24 of the Residence Act (§ 24 AufenthG) are based on an EU decision and have been extended several times, most recently until 4 March 2027. The EU and Germany are discussing how to handle the time afterwards, including a move to other residence permits. Follow official announcements and do not rely on rumours in social media groups. Our guide to the residence permit explains the current rules.

Important: as long as you hold a valid permit under § 24, you do not have to change anything. Changing to another permit is a step to plan carefully, ideally with advice, because returning to § 24 later may not be possible.

Why to plan now

Most other residence permits require things that take time: German language skills, recognised qualifications, a job contract, enough income or a training place. If you start now, you will meet more conditions when the moment comes. The same steps also improve your life in Germany regardless of the legal question: a better job, more independence and better schooling for the children.

Permits for work

People with a job can switch to a work permit if they meet its conditions. The main options are:

  • Skilled workers with vocational training or a degree (§ 18a, § 18b AufenthG): you need a recognised or comparable qualification and a suitable job offer.
  • EU Blue Card (§ 18g): for university graduates with a job above a salary threshold, lower in shortage occupations such as IT, engineering, medicine and nursing.
  • Experienced workers: in some cases, professional experience and a job with a minimum salary are enough without formal recognition.

Recognition of your Ukrainian diploma is the key for many of these; see our guide to recognition.

Permits for training and study

Young people and adults can get a permit for vocational training (§ 16a) or study (§ 16b). After successful training or a degree, they can switch to a work permit and search for a job. A training contract with a company in Upper Franconia is therefore a strong basis for staying. Read our guides to vocational training and studying.

Family permits

If you marry a German citizen or a person with a long-term permit, or your child is German, family permits may be possible. Children who grow up and go to school in Germany can, under certain conditions, receive their own residence permit after several years, especially if they integrate well at school (§ 25a). These rules are detailed and depend on the individual case.

The settlement permit

A settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) is unlimited and gives more security. As a rule, it requires several years of residence with a permit, a secure livelihood without benefits, pension contributions, German skills and knowledge of the legal and social order, and sufficient living space. Skilled workers and Blue Card holders can get it faster. Whether and how years under temporary protection count depends on the type of permit; ask the immigration office or a counselling service.

Citizenship

Since 2024, naturalisation in Germany is possible after five years of lawful residence, and Germany now allows dual citizenship. You need German at level B1, the naturalisation test, a secure livelihood, no relevant criminal convictions and a commitment to the free democratic order. Ukrainian law has its own rules on dual citizenship, so check the consequences for your Ukrainian citizenship before applying.

What you can do now

  • Learn German: complete the integration course and aim for B1, later B2. Keep all certificates.
  • Get your diploma recognised: apply for recognition even if you do not need it for your current job.
  • Work with a proper contract: employment subject to social insurance builds pension contributions and shows a secure livelihood.
  • Keep documents valid: renew Ukrainian passports in time; see our guide to Ukrainian documents.
  • Collect proof: keep employment contracts, wage slips, tax notices, rental contracts and school reports in one folder.
  • Avoid debts and criminal records: both can block later permits.

Children and young people

Children benefit most from stability. Good school results, a school-leaving certificate and German skills open the way to training, study and their own residence permit. Make sure they attend school regularly and get support early when they struggle; see our guide to school and Kita.

Applying for a change

A change of permit is applied for at the immigration office (Ausländerbehörde), in Upper Franconia at the district office or the city. Apply in good time before your current permit expires. While the office decides, you usually receive a fictional certificate (Fiktionsbescheinigung) that keeps your current rights. Bring complete documents; missing papers are the most common reason for delays.

Returning to Ukraine

Many people want to return when it is safe. A return does not have to be either now or never: some keep links to both countries, work remotely or prepare a return step by step. Before longer trips to Ukraine, check how they affect your German permit and benefits. Return counselling services advise free of charge and sometimes support returnees.

Beware of false promises

Some agencies promise guaranteed permits or citizenship for money. No private company can guarantee a decision of the immigration office. Use free counselling services of welfare organisations and migration counselling, and check every offer carefully.

Language certificates that count

For residence permits and citizenship, only recognised certificates count, such as the final test of the integration course (Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer), telc, Goethe-Institut or ÖSD exams. A course attendance confirmation is not enough. Keep the original certificates safe and make copies. If you passed the Leben in Deutschland test in the integration course, it can also replace the naturalisation test.

How we help

We help you understand your options, prepare documents, find German courses, start recognition and book appointments at the immigration office. For complex cases, we connect you with migration counselling or a specialist lawyer. Ask for help.

Frequently asked questions

How long does temporary protection last?

Currently until 4 March 2027. Follow official announcements for decisions on the time afterwards.

Do I have to change my permit now?

No. With a valid § 24 permit you do not have to change anything; plan a switch carefully with advice.

Which permit is possible with a job?

Often a skilled worker permit or EU Blue Card, if your qualification is recognised and the job fits.

When can I become a German citizen?

As a rule after five years of lawful residence, with B1 German, the test and a secure livelihood.

What can I do today to prepare?

Learn German, get your diploma recognised, work with a proper contract and keep your documents valid.
